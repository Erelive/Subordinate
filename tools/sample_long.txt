Speech recognition on a personal computer used to require sending audio to a remote server, waiting for a response, and hoping the network stayed fast enough that the delay did not become obvious to the listener. Modern open weight models changed that calculation completely, because a mid range laptop graphics card can now transcribe speech considerably faster than a person can produce it, which means the entire round trip disappears and the only remaining delay is the time the model itself needs to think. The interesting engineering problem is no longer accuracy, it is the fact that these models expect to see a complete recording before they answer, while a live caption system must show something useful before the sentence has finished. The usual solution is to transcribe an overlapping window again and again, and to display a word only once two consecutive attempts have produced exactly the same result, which keeps the visible text from rewriting itself while the speaker is still talking. Everything that has not yet been confirmed twice is shown in a dimmer colour, so a reader can tell at a glance which words are settled and which are still a guess that the system may revise a moment later.
